How to Validate a Digital Signature in Adobe Acrobat Reader

By PDFSign  ·  Sep 2026  ·  5 min read
Home › Blog › Validate Signature in Acrobat Reader

You received (or sent) a digitally signed PDF — a tender document, an invoice, a verification certificate — and now you need to prove the signature is genuine. The green tick in Adobe Acrobat Reader is that proof. This guide shows exactly where to find it, what each status means, and how to fix the two most common problems along the way.

Why Acrobat Reader? Chrome, Edge and most phone viewers display a signed PDF but do not verify it. Adobe Acrobat Reader is the free, universally accepted validator — it checks the signature mathematically and against the certificate authority.

Validate a signature in 5 steps

1Open the PDF in Adobe Acrobat Reader

Right-click the file → Open with → Adobe Acrobat Reader. If you don't have it, download the free Reader from adobe.com. Make sure it's a recent version — old versions can fail to display images and signatures in modern PDFs.

2Read the blue bar at the top

Acrobat immediately verifies every signature and shows the verdict in a bar across the top: "Signed and all signatures are valid" with a green tick ✓ is the result you want.

3Open the Signature Panel

Click Signature Panel on that bar (or View → Show/Hide → Navigation Panes → Signatures). It lists every signature with the signer's name, signing time, and — critically — whether the document has been modified since it was signed.

4Check who signed

Right-click the signature (on the page or in the panel) → Show Signature Properties → Show Signer's Certificate. Confirm the signer's name and that the certificate was issued by a licensed Indian CA (eMudhra, Capricorn, VSign, PantaSign etc. under CCA India).

5Trust CCA India — once

If the signature shows "Validity Unknown" with a question mark, Acrobat simply hasn't loaded India's certificate chain yet. Go to Edit → Preferences → Trust Manager and click Update Now under "Automatic Adobe Approved Trust List (AATL) updates". Reopen the PDF — the question mark becomes a green tick, permanently, for all Indian DSC-signed documents.

What each status means

✅
Green tick — ValidSignature verified, certificate trusted, document unchanged since signing. Safe to rely on.
❓
Question mark — Validity UnknownThe signature is intact but the viewer doesn't trust the CA chain yet. Fix with the AATL update in Step 5.
❌
Red X — InvalidThe document was altered after signing, or the certificate is revoked. Do not rely on it — request a fresh signed copy.

Images or the signature stamp not showing?

Occasionally a signed PDF opens in Acrobat with a photo, seal, or stamp missing from the page — while the same file looks fine in Edge or Chrome. This is almost always the viewer, not the document:

  • Update Acrobat Reader — Help → Check for Updates. Outdated versions can't render images in PDFs produced by modern tools and portals. This fixes the vast majority of cases.
  • Check "Show large images" — Edit → Preferences → Page Display → make sure Show large images is ticked. When off, Acrobat silently hides photos and scans.
Good to know: a signature that verifies with a green tick is legally valid under the IT Act 2000 even if a viewer has display quirks — the cryptographic verification is about the document's bytes, not how a particular app draws the page.
