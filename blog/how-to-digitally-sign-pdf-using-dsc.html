How to Digitally Sign a PDF Using DSC — Step by Step

1Install PDFSign.in and launch it

Download PDFSign from the Microsoft Store or direct download. Install and open it. No account creation or login required.

2Plug in your DSC USB token

Insert the token into a USB port. Wait 3–5 seconds for Windows to recognise it. If your token manager software shows a connected/ready status, you're set. If it's your first time using the token on this PC, install the token driver first.

3Select "Digital Signature (DSC)" mode

In PDFSign, under SIGNING MODE at the top, click "✦ Digital Signature (DSC)". The interface switches to show DSC-specific fields: CERTIFICATE, REASON, LOCATION, and a signature stamp preview.

4Choose your certificate

In the CERTIFICATE dropdown, your DSC certificate appears automatically — showing your name or your organisation's name as issued by your CA. If it does not appear, click the "↻ Refresh" button. Select the correct certificate if you have more than one (e.g. personal vs. company DSC).

💡 Certificate shows Name, Issuer, and Valid Until. After selection, PDFSign displays the certificate's details. Verify the name matches your expected DSC and that the expiry date is in the future before signing.
5Enter Reason and Location

In REASON, type the purpose of signing — "Authorised Signatory", "Approved", "Certified by CA", or "Signed for tender submission". In LOCATION, type your city or district. These details appear inside the signature field when the PDF is opened in Adobe Reader — adding legal and professional context.

6Select your input PDF and output path

Under DOCUMENT, click Browse next to INPUT PDF and select your file. PDFSign automatically creates the output path with _signed appended (e.g. Tender_Doc_signed.pdf). Your original PDF is never modified.

7Set signature position and enable timestamp

Use the 3×3 position grid under SIGNATURE POSITION to choose where the signature stamp appears on the page — most tender and government submissions expect the signature in the bottom-right of the last page. Confirm that "Include RFC 3161 timestamp" is checked (it is by default — leave it on).

8Click "Sign PDF" and enter your PIN

Click the green "✦ Sign PDF" button. A Windows security dialog prompts for your DSC token PIN. Enter the 4–8 digit PIN and click OK. PDFSign signs the document in 2–5 seconds. A success message appears. Your signed PDF is saved at the output path.

✅ Verification in Adobe Reader: Open the signed PDF in Adobe Reader. A blue banner reads "Signed and all signatures are valid" with your name, CA, and timestamp. This is what government portals and verifiers check.

Why Avoid Adobe Acrobat for DSC Signing?

Adobe Acrobat can sign PDFs with DSC tokens, but there are four practical problems:

  1. High cost: Acrobat Standard or Pro costs ₹1,300–₹1,800/month just to access PDF signing features. PDFSign.in starts at ₹99/month.
  2. Complex DSC setup: Acrobat requires you to manually configure a "Digital ID" — locating your token's PKCS#11 library file (.dll), pointing Acrobat to it, and setting it as the default. This breaks across Acrobat and Windows updates.
  3. No batch signing in standard plans: Signing a folder of 50 PDFs in Acrobat requires Acrobat DC plus Action Wizard or scripting. PDFSign does this natively in 2 clicks.
  4. No built-in RFC 3161 timestamp: Acrobat's timestamp support requires manual TSA server configuration. PDFSign includes Sectigo/Comodo RFC 3161 timestamp by default, at no extra cost.

Troubleshooting: Certificate Not Showing Up

If your certificate doesn't appear in the PDFSign dropdown after clicking Refresh:

  1. Open Windows Certificate Manager: Press Win+R, type certmgr.msc, and press Enter. Navigate to Personal → Certificates. Your DSC certificate should be listed there. If not, your token driver is not installed correctly.
  2. Reinstall your token middleware. Download the latest driver from your CA's website (emudhra.com, proxkey.com, hyp.co.in, etc.).
  3. Try a different USB port — some USB hubs cause detection issues.
  4. Restart PDFSign after plugging in the token.

How do I create my digital signature in a PDF using DSC?
A digital signature using DSC is not drawn or typed — it is cryptographically created from your Class 2 or Class 3 DSC certificate stored on your USB token. PDFSign.in reads the certificate from the token and creates a tamper-evident digital signature block in the PDF containing your name, issuing CA, date/time, and RFC 3161 timestamp.
Which DSC tokens work with PDFSign.in?
All PKCS#11-compatible USB DSC tokens used in India: eMudhra (ePass 2003, ePass 3003), HyperPKI HYP2003, Capricorn CA, ProxKey, Feitian, WD Pro, Watchdata, SafeNet. Any Class 2 or Class 3 DSC issued by a licensed Certifying Authority under CCA India is supported.
Is a PDF digitally signed with DSC legally valid in India?
Yes. A digital signature applied using a Class 2 or Class 3 DSC is legally valid under the IT Act 2000 and IT Amendment Act 2008. PDFSign.in creates PAdES-compliant signatures accepted on GeM, CPPP, NIC eProcurement, MCA21, GST portal, and all major Indian government portals.
What is RFC 3161 timestamp and do I need it?
RFC 3161 embeds a cryptographic proof of the signing time from a trusted authority (Sectigo/Comodo). It keeps your digital signature legally valid even after your DSC certificate expires.
